Brandon is one of Tampa Bay's most established suburban communities, originally developed in the 1960s-1970s and growing steadily through the 1990s and 2000s. Unlike Riverview's predominantly newer construction, Brandon features a genuinely diverse mix of housing styles and eras. Older neighborhoods along Lumsden Road and near the Westfield Brandon Mall feature concrete block homes from the 1970s-1980s that may have original wood fascia, aluminum soffit, and flat or low-pitched roofs requiring specialized exterior preparation.

The Bloomingdale area bridges Brandon and Valrico with 1990s-2000s construction similar to Riverview's newer stock. Brandon's commercial corridor along SR-60 and the Westfield Mall area provides substantial commercial painting opportunities including retail refreshes, restaurant repaints, and office suite turnovers. The area's mature tree canopy in older neighborhoods creates more shade and moisture conditions than Riverview's newer, more open developments, affecting exterior paint longevity and mildew management.

Local Climate

Brandon-Specific Painting Considerations

Brandon shares Tampa Bay's subtropical climate with Riverview but has distinctive micro-conditions that affect painting. Brandon's older, more established neighborhoods feature significantly more mature tree canopy than Riverview's newer developments. While shade reduces UV damage on exterior surfaces (a positive), it also creates more persistent moisture conditions that encourage mildew growth, particularly on north-facing walls and where landscaping grows close to the structure.

Homes in Brandon's older neighborhoods built in the 1970s-1980s often have less effective drainage and ventilation than modern construction, making moisture management a critical consideration during exterior prep. We address this by using mildew-resistant primers and topcoats on shaded surfaces and recommending homeowners maintain 12-18 inches of clearance between landscaping and painted surfaces.
