The Complete Guide to Cabinet Painting in Florida (2026)

By Eliseo Ayala • Ayala Pro Painting • June 1, 2026 • 9 min read

Cabinet painting is one of the highest-impact, most cost-effective home improvement projects available to Florida homeowners, delivering a dramatic kitchen or bathroom transformation at 70-80% less than the cost of full cabinet replacement. Professional cabinet refinishing in the Tampa Bay area typically costs $3,500 to $10,000 depending on kitchen size, and takes 5-7 days to complete. This guide covers everything Florida homeowners need to know about painting cabinets, from product selection for humidity resistance to the professional process that delivers factory-smooth results.

Paint vs. Replace — When Is Cabinet Painting the Right Choice?

Painting is the right call when your cabinet boxes and doors are structurally sound, the layout works for you, and you simply want a fresh color or finish. Replacement makes more sense when cabinets are water-damaged or failing, when you want to change the layout, or when you want an entirely different door style that can't be achieved by refinishing.

The cost difference is dramatic. Refinishing a kitchen typically runs $3,500 to $10,000, while full replacement runs $15,000 to $40,000 or more. Refinishing also takes about 5 to 7 days versus 3 to 6 weeks for replacement — and your kitchen stays largely usable throughout.

The Professional Cabinet Painting Process

A professional refinish is a multi-step process, and each step exists for a reason:

  • Remove and label every door, drawer front, and piece of hardware so it all goes back exactly where it belongs
  • Clean and degrease thoroughly — kitchen grease is invisible but it will prevent paint from bonding if it isn't removed
  • Sand all surfaces to give the primer something to grip
  • Apply a bonding primer formulated to stick to slick, previously finished cabinets
  • Spray multiple coats of enamel for a smooth, factory-like finish with no brush marks
  • Reassemble once fully cured, reinstalling doors, drawers, and hardware

When you interview a cabinet painter, ask whether they remove the doors (they should), whether they spray or brush (spraying gives the smoothest finish), and which primer they use. The answers separate professionals from weekend operators.

Best Paint Products for Florida Kitchen Cabinets

Cabinet finishes take far more abuse than walls, so cabinet-grade products matter. We rely on Sherwin-Williams ProClassic and Emerald Urethane Trim Enamel and Benjamin Moore Advance and Cabinet Coat. These products are harder, self-leveling, chip-resistant, and moisture-resistant — qualities ordinary wall paint simply doesn't have. In Florida's humidity, product quality matters even more, because cheaper cabinet paints are prone to yellowing over time.

Professional pricing includes all the prep, priming, multiple enamel coats, and reassembly. For comparison, DIY materials alone run $500 to $1,500 — and the results rarely match a sprayed, properly prepped professional finish.

Can You DIY Cabinet Painting?

Technically yes, but cabinet refinishing is one of the hardest DIY projects to pull off well. The challenges are achieving a smooth finish without spray equipment, degreasing thoroughly enough, choosing and applying the right bonding primer, allowing adequate dry time between coats, and avoiding drips and brush marks on every door.

The most common DIY cabinet failure is peeling within 6 to 12 months — almost always caused by inadequate degreasing or using the wrong primer.

Cabinet Painting and Florida Humidity

Kitchens are humid places to begin with — cooking, dishwashers, and limited ventilation all add moisture — and Florida amplifies the challenge. That's why cabinet finishes should be enamel-based rather than latex wall paint, why we ventilate during and after the project, why we allow extra dry time between coats, and why a semi-gloss or satin sheen is ideal for wiping down and resisting moisture.
