Preparing Your Home for Exterior Painting in Florida

By Eliseo Ayala • Ayala Pro Painting • June 1, 2026 • 8 min read

Preparing your Florida home for an exterior painting project involves two types of preparation: what the homeowner should do before the painting crew arrives, and what the professional painters handle as part of their service. As a homeowner, your main responsibilities are clearing access to your home's exterior, securing landscaping and outdoor items, notifying your HOA if applicable, and coordinating parking and pet access. The painting crew handles all surface preparation including power washing, scraping, sanding, caulking, priming, and protection of surrounding surfaces.

What Homeowners Should Do Before Painters Arrive

Your job before the crew shows up is to clear the way and protect what's yours:

  • Move vehicles at least 15 feet from the walls being painted
  • Clear patio furniture, grills, potted plants, and decorations from the perimeter
  • Trim landscaping back 18-24 inches from the house (this also helps paint last longer)
  • Note any delicate plants that can't be moved so the crew can protect them
  • Remove wall-mounted items: hose reels, decorative pieces, address numbers
  • Make sure the crew can reach exterior electrical outlets for equipment
  • Arrange pet access — keep pets inside or in a safe area during work hours
  • Share alarm codes, gate access, or locked-area details with your painter
  • If you're in an HOA, confirm color approval is finalized before the start date

What Professional Painters Handle (You Don't Need to Do This)

Many homeowners over-prepare because they don't realize how much is included. Your crew handles power washing every surface, scraping loose and peeling paint, sanding for smooth transitions, filling cracks and holes (stucco patching and wood filling), caulking around windows, doors, and penetrations, priming bare and repaired areas, protecting landscaping with drop cloths, masking windows and fixtures, and shielding walkways and driveways from overspray. That's a substantial amount of skilled work — and it's where a paint job is won or lost.

Florida-Specific Timing: When to Schedule Exterior Painting

Timing matters more in Florida than almost anywhere. October through April is the sweet spot — the dry season brings lower humidity, fewer storms, and ideal curing conditions. May and early June still work well with early-morning starts ahead of afternoon storms. July through September, peak hurricane season, is the most weather-dependent stretch and tends to bring more delays. Because dry-season demand is high, book four to six weeks ahead. That said, if your exterior is actively failing, don't wait — professionals can manage weather windows year-round.

The Professional Exterior Prep Process Explained

Our exterior prep is a seven-step process, and prep makes up 40 to 60 percent of the total project time for good reason — it's the single biggest factor in how long your paint lasts:

  • Power wash to remove oxidation, mildew, and dirt so paint can bond
  • Scrape and sand failing paint and feather the edges smooth
  • Caulk and seal every gap, because each one is a moisture entry point
  • Repair stucco cracks, damaged wood, and fascia
  • Prime bare and repaired surfaces with a bonding primer
  • First coat, working top to bottom and keeping a wet edge
  • Second coat and detail work for full, uniform coverage

What to Expect During the Painting Process

A typical single-story exterior takes three to five days; a two-story runs five to seven. Power washing day is the loudest; the painting days are relatively quiet. The crew needs clear access to all sides of the home and will give you daily updates on progress and anything they discover during prep. If rain rolls in, work pauses and resumes once surfaces are dry. Expect a clean job site each day and a thorough cleanup at the end.

How to Maintain Your Exterior Paint After the Project

A little maintenance protects your investment. Pressure wash annually to keep mildew and algae from gaining a foothold, inspect caulking each year and address gaps early, keep landscaping trimmed off the walls, and fix small chips or peeling right away — a $50 touch-up can prevent a $5,000 repaint. Watch for stucco cracks after storms and repair them promptly. With this kind of upkeep, a professional Florida exterior should last seven to ten years.
