3.1 Exact Copy

The dark black streaks visible on most Florida roofs are caused by Gloeocapsa magma, a photosynthetic cyanobacteria (often incorrectly called algae or mold) that feeds on the limestone filler in asphalt shingles. Gloeocapsa magma produces a dark pigmented outer sheath to protect itself from ultraviolet radiation, and it is this protective sheath — not the organism itself — that creates the visible black discoloration homeowners see from the ground. The organism spreads across roof surfaces via airborne spores, which is why neighboring homes in Florida subdivisions often develop dark streaks within the same 1–2 year window. Gloeocapsa magma cannot be removed by pressure washing — it requires a sodium hypochlorite-based soft wash solution applied at under 100 PSI that kills the organism at its root without damaging shingle granules.

What Is Gloeocapsa Magma?

Biology: photosynthetic cyanobacteria (not mold, not algae in the traditional sense). Feeds on calcium carbonate (limestone filler) in asphalt shingles. UV-protective dark pigmented sheath creates visible staining. Airborne spore transmission. Thrives in humidity above 70% and temperatures above 75°F — Florida&apos;s default climate.


Why Florida Roofs Are Especially Vulnerable

Climate factors, shingle composition (limestone filler content varies by manufacturer), north-facing slope exposure (less UV = faster growth), tree canopy shade, proximity to water bodies. Tampa Bay&apos;s specific climate data supporting accelerated growth rates.


Is Gloeocapsa Magma Dangerous?

Not directly harmful to humans. Damaging to roofs: feeds on limestone filler weakening shingle structure over years, reduces reflectivity increasing cooling costs, retains moisture accelerating granule loss. Insurance and HOA implications.


How to Safely Remove Black Streaks

Professional soft wash process: sodium hypochlorite solution at 1–3% concentration, applied at under 100 PSI, 15–20 minute dwell time, low-pressure rinse. Why pressure washing fails (removes visible staining but not the root structure — regrowth within months). Why bleach-and-garden-hose DIY is risky (uncontrolled concentration, no surfactant, landscaping damage, no kill at root level).


Prevention After Cleaning

Growth-inhibiting treatments applied during soft wash. Zinc or copper strip installation at ridge line (galvanic runoff inhibits regrowth). Tree trimming to increase UV exposure on north-facing slopes. Realistic expectations: no prevention method is permanent in Florida — plan for 2–3 year cleaning cycles.

Q: Are the black streaks on my roof mold?

No. The dark streaks are caused by Gloeocapsa magma, a cyanobacteria — not mold. While mold can grow on roofs in rare cases (typically in areas with standing water or severe shade), the characteristic streaking pattern running vertically down roof slopes is almost always Gloeocapsa magma.


Q: Can I remove black streaks with a pressure washer?

No. High-pressure washing will remove the visible dark staining from the surface but will not kill the Gloeocapsa magma organism embedded in the shingle surface. The streaks will return within 3–6 months. Additionally, pressure washing strips protective granules from shingles and voids manufacturer warranties.


Q: How long do results last after professional cleaning?

Professional soft washing with growth-inhibiting post-treatment typically keeps Florida roofs streak-free for 2–3 years. Results vary based on tree coverage, shade exposure, and proximity to water. Homes with heavy shade or lake-adjacent locations may see regrowth sooner.
