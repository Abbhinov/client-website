3.1 Exact Copy

Florida pavers should be professionally cleaned and resealed every 2–4 years depending on the sealer type, sun exposure level, and foot traffic intensity. The two most common paver sealer categories are penetrating sealers (invisible, natural-look finish that protects from within the paver without changing appearance) and film-forming sealers (wet-look or semi-gloss finish that enhances color depth and creates a visible surface coating). Film-forming sealers in Florida&apos;s intense UV environment typically last 2–3 years before requiring strip-and-reapply, while penetrating sealers last 3–5 years. Critically, pavers must be professionally cleaned before any sealer application — sealing over dirty pavers locks in stains, organic growth, and discoloration permanently.

Why Seal Pavers in Florida?

UV color fading prevention, stain resistance (oil, leaf tannins, rust), weed and ant inhibition in joints, moisture resistance reducing efflorescence, enhanced appearance (color depth). Florida-specific: UV intensity in Tampa Bay accelerates color fading 2–3x faster than northern states.


Penetrating vs. Film-Forming Sealers

Comparison table: appearance (natural vs. enhanced), durability (3–5 years vs. 2–3 years), maintenance (reapply vs. strip-and-reapply), cost ($0.50–$1.00/sqft vs. $1.00–$2.00/sqft), best for (homeowners who prefer natural look vs. those who want enhanced color).


The Clean-Sand-Seal Process

3-step process: 1) Pressure clean to remove all organic growth and staining, 2) Replace polymeric sand in joints (prevent weeds and ants), 3) Apply sealer after 24–48 hour drying period. Why all three steps are necessary — skipping any one compromises the result.


How to Tell Your Pavers Need Resealing

Visual indicators: water no longer beads on surface (penetrating sealer worn), glossy finish has dulled or worn in traffic paths (film-forming), weed growth in joints (polymeric sand deteriorated), color has noticeably faded compared to shaded areas.


Common Paver Sealing Mistakes

Sealing without cleaning first, applying sealer too thick, not allowing adequate drying time before sealing (moisture trapped under sealer causes white haze), using the wrong sealer type for the paver material, applying sealer in direct sunlight or high humidity.

Q: How much does paver sealing cost in Florida?

Professional paver cleaning and sealing in the Tampa Bay area typically costs $1.50–$3.00 per square foot for the complete clean-sand-seal process. A standard 400 sq ft pool deck would cost $600–$1,200. Price varies based on paver condition, sealer type selected, and whether polymeric sand replacement is needed.


Q: Can I seal pavers myself?

While DIY paver sealing is possible, it is not recommended. The most common DIY mistake is applying sealer over dirty or damp pavers, which locks in stains and creates a white haze that requires professional stripping to fix. Professional application ensures proper cleaning, drying time, and even sealer coverage.


Q: Should I seal new pavers?

New pavers should be allowed to cure and weather for at least 60–90 days before sealing. This allows efflorescence (natural mineral deposits) to work its way to the surface and be cleaned off before sealing. Sealing too early traps efflorescence under the sealer, creating a white cloudy appearance.
