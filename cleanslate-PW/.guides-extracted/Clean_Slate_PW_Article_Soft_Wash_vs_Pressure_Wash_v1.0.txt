3.1 Exact Copy

Soft washing and pressure washing are two fundamentally different exterior cleaning methods that serve different surface types. Soft washing uses low-pressure water at 100–500 PSI combined with biodegradable cleaning agents to safely clean delicate surfaces like asphalt shingle roofs, stucco siding, painted wood, vinyl, and lanai screens. Pressure washing uses high-pressure water at 1,500–4,000 PSI to physically blast embedded dirt, oil, and stains from durable surfaces like concrete driveways, sidewalks, brick pavers, and pool decks. Choosing the wrong method for the wrong surface is the single most common cause of exterior cleaning damage in Florida — high-pressure wands strip shingle granules, crack stucco, and tear screen mesh, while soft washing alone cannot remove deeply embedded oil stains and tire marks from concrete.

Q: Is soft washing better than pressure washing?

Neither is universally better — each is designed for different surfaces. Soft washing is required for roofs, siding, stucco, and screens. Pressure washing is required for concrete and pavers. The best cleaning companies offer both methods and select the right one based on the surface type.


Q: Can I soft wash my driveway?

Soft washing alone will not effectively clean a concrete driveway. The low pressure cannot remove embedded oil stains, tire marks, or deeply ground-in dirt from concrete pores. Driveways need the mechanical force of high-pressure surface cleaning (2,500–4,000 PSI) preceded by a chemical pre-treatment for best results.


Q: Will pressure washing damage my roof?

Yes — high-pressure washing will damage almost any residential roof. It strips protective granules from asphalt shingles, cracks concrete and clay tiles, and voids manufacturer warranties. Roofs should only be cleaned using soft wash methods at under 100 PSI.
