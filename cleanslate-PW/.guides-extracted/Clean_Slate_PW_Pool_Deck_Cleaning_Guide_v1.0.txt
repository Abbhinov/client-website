Section 8: Our Process (5 Steps)

Numbered 5-step process showing exactly how the pool deck &amp; paver cleaning service is performed. Each step has a bold heading and 1–2 sentence description. This section builds trust through transparency — homeowners want to know what will happen on their property before they commit.


8.1 Process Steps

Step 1 — Surface assessment: We identify the deck material (pavers, travertine, concrete, cool-deck) and calibrate pressure and cleaning chemistry accordingly. Pool furniture is moved or covered.

Step 2 — Pre-treatment: Cleaning solution is applied to break down algae, mildew, and organic staining. Solution type varies by surface — acidic cleaners for mineral deposits on travertine, alkaline for organic growth on concrete.

Step 3 — Pressure cleaning: Surface cleaned at the appropriate PSI for the material. Pavers and travertine receive lower pressure (1,500–2,500 PSI) than poured concrete (2,500–3,500 PSI).

Step 4 — Joint work (pavers): Old sand is removed from paver joints. New polymeric sand is swept into joints and activated with water to harden into a weed-resistant, ant-proof barrier.

Step 5 — Sealer application (optional): A breathable penetrating sealer or wet-look enhancing sealer is applied to protect against UV fading, staining, and moisture absorption. Sealer type selected based on homeowner preference and material compatibility.

Section 13: FAQ Section

Five questions specific to pool deck &amp; paver cleaning. These must match the FAQPage JSON-LD schema exactly.


Q: Should I seal my pavers after cleaning?

Sealing is optional but highly recommended in Florida. A quality paver sealer protects against UV color fading, makes future cleaning easier by preventing deep stain absorption, inhibits weed and moss growth in joints, and enhances the color depth of the pavers. We offer both matte (natural look) and wet-look (enhanced color) sealer options.


Q: How often should I clean my pool deck?

Most Tampa Bay pool decks benefit from professional cleaning every 12–18 months. Pool decks under heavy tree canopy or with north-facing shade may need cleaning annually. Between professional cleanings, a simple garden hose rinse every few weeks helps prevent organic buildup.


Q: Can you clean travertine without damaging it?

Yes. Travertine requires lower pressure (under 2,000 PSI) and specific pH-neutral or mildly acidic cleaning agents that will not etch the calcium carbonate surface. We have extensive experience with travertine pool decks throughout the Tampa Bay area and adjust our approach based on the finish type (tumbled, honed, polished).


Q: What is polymeric sand and why do I need it?

Polymeric sand is a jointing sand that contains binding agents. When activated with water, it hardens in the paver joints to resist washout from rain, prevent weed germination, and block ant infiltration. Standard sand washes out within months in Florida&apos;s rainfall. Polymeric sand lasts 3–5 years between replacements.


Q: Do you move pool furniture before cleaning?

Yes. Our team moves all portable pool furniture, planters, and smaller items before cleaning and replaces them afterward. For built-in features like outdoor kitchens, fire pits, or permanently mounted furniture, we clean around them with detail wands.
