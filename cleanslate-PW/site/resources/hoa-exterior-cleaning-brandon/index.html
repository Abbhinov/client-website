HOA-Approved Exterior Cleaning: What Brandon Homeowners Need to Know

By Jason Caldwell, Clean Slate Pressure Washing Published April 1, 2026 5 min read

In This Guide

  1. Common HOA Violations Related to Exterior Cleaning
  2. How to Respond to a Violation Notice
  3. Tampa Bay HOA Communities with Strict Standards
  4. Proactive Maintenance to Avoid Violations
  5. How Clean Slate PW Handles HOA Jobs

Hillsborough County HOA communities routinely issue exterior maintenance violation notices for visible algae on roofs, mildew on siding, stained driveways, and discolored fences. Common HOA-governed neighborhoods in the Brandon, Valrico, Riverview, and FishHawk areas enforce exterior cleaning standards through quarterly or annual visual inspections conducted by management companies or volunteer architectural review committees. If you receive a violation notice, you typically have 14–30 days to correct the issue before fines begin — response timelines vary by community covenant. Professional exterior cleaning resolves the most common violations in a single visit, and reputable cleaning companies provide before-and-after photo documentation formatted for HOA compliance submission.

Common HOA Violations Related to Exterior Cleaning

Across Hillsborough County HOA communities, five exterior cleaning-related violations account for the overwhelming majority of notices issued. The most common is visible algae or dark streaking on roofs — usually Gloeocapsa magma — which appears in notices as language like "roof discoloration not consistent with community standards" or "visible algae growth on roofing material." Correction timelines for roof violations are typically 30 days. The second most common is mildew or organic growth on siding or stucco, often phrased as "discolored exterior walls" or "visible mildew on siding panels." Correction timeline: usually 14–30 days.

Third is stained driveways and walkways — oil drips, organic mildew, rust from irrigation, and general discoloration. Notice language: "driveway and walkway surfaces in disrepair." Fourth is discolored fences, particularly white vinyl that has gone gray-green. Notice language: "perimeter fencing not maintained to community standards." Fifth is dirty lanai screens or pool cage enclosures, which are visible from neighboring properties and increasingly cited as community-standard violations. Each of these can typically be resolved with a single professional cleaning visit. See our service pages for roof soft washing, house soft washing, driveway cleaning, fence cleaning, and lanai cleaning.

How to Respond to a Violation Notice

The most important thing when you receive a violation notice is to read it carefully. Identify (1) the specific violation cited, (2) the correction deadline, (3) the specific evidence the HOA is requesting (photos, third-party invoice, inspection sign-off), and (4) the fine schedule if you miss the deadline. Most Hillsborough County HOAs follow a graduated fine structure: an initial $25–$100 fine after the first missed deadline, escalating to $100–$250 per week of continued non-compliance, with severe cases reaching $1,000+ or lien threats.

The recommended response sequence: Step 1. Request a free cleaning estimate within 24–48 hours of receiving the notice — most reputable companies respond same-day. Step 2. Schedule the service before the correction deadline. Most professional cleaning companies in Brandon can accommodate 3–5 business day turnaround, with priority scheduling available for HOA deadlines. Step 3. Have the cleaning company photograph the work before-and-after. Step 4. Submit the photo documentation to your HOA or management company with the original violation reference number. Step 5. Keep records of the cleaning invoice, the photo documentation, and the HOA's acknowledgment of compliance. These records protect you in future disputes about repeated violations or covenant enforcement.

Tampa Bay HOA Communities with Strict Standards

Not all Hillsborough County HOAs enforce exterior cleaning with the same intensity. The strictest are master-planned communities with active architectural review committees and dedicated management company oversight. FishHawk Ranch is among the most rigorous in the region, with quarterly community-wide inspections and formal notice processes for any visible algae, mildew, or staining. Panther Trace in Riverview follows a similar quarterly cycle. Heritage Isles in Tampa enforces exterior maintenance via dedicated covenant committee inspections. Bloomingdale Hills and the broader Bloomingdale community conduct annual inspections with mid-year drive-throughs.

In the Brandon area, enforcement varies dramatically by neighborhood. Communities like Heather Lakes, Sedgefield, and Brandon Lakes conduct active enforcement. Non-HOA neighborhoods like Oakfield and Town 'N Country Park have no exterior standards. The Valrico HOA landscape is similar — Buckhorn Estates and Coventry Park enforce actively, while older non-HOA sections operate without restrictions. This is not an exhaustive list. To determine your specific community's enforcement intensity, request a copy of your CC&Rs (Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions) from your HOA board or management company.

Proactive Maintenance to Avoid Violations

The least expensive way to handle HOA exterior maintenance enforcement is to never receive a violation notice in the first place. An annual exterior cleaning schedule — typically house wash plus driveway in one visit — keeps most homes well within compliance standards year-round. For homes with heavier exposure (north-facing slopes, heavy tree canopy, lakefront properties), a 9–10 month cleaning interval is more appropriate. Roofs need cleaning every 2–3 years in most Florida HOAs to stay ahead of Gloeocapsa magma streaking.

Group pricing is another option that's underutilized in Hillsborough County HOAs. Many cleaning companies, including ours, offer 10–20% group discounts when 4+ neighboring homes book service in the same day or week. Coordinate with neighbors and your HOA's social committee to organize a "community cleaning week" — particularly useful in advance of HOA-scheduled inspection periods. Set calendar reminders 30–45 days before your community's typical inspection schedule so you can clean before the inspector arrives. Finally, document your cleaning history with dated invoices and before/after photos. If an aggressive enforcement situation ever arises, a multi-year history of professional cleaning is the strongest defense against escalation.
