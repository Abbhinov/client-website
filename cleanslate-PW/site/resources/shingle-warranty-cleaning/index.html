Will Pressure Washing Void Your Shingle Warranty? Manufacturer Requirements Explained

By Jason Caldwell, Clean Slate Pressure Washing Published April 1, 2026 5 min read

In This Guide

  1. What Major Manufacturers Say
  2. Why High Pressure Damages Shingles
  3. How to Verify Your Specific Warranty
  4. What Soft Washing Does Differently
  5. Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Roof Cleaner

Most major asphalt shingle manufacturers — including GAF, CertainTeed, Owens Corning, and Atlas — explicitly require that roof cleaning be performed using low-pressure methods only. High-pressure washing (above 100–300 PSI depending on manufacturer) voids the limited warranty coverage by causing granule loss, shingle distortion, and moisture channel creation that accelerates deterioration. This means homeowners who hire a contractor that pressure washes their roof with a standard 2,500–4,000 PSI wand are not only risking physical damage — they are voluntarily voiding their manufacturer warranty, which can represent $5,000–$15,000 in lost coverage on a typical Florida roof.

What Major Manufacturers Say

GAF, the largest asphalt shingle manufacturer in North America, publishes a cleaning maintenance bulletin that explicitly recommends low-pressure cleaning methods only. GAF's guidance specifies that high-pressure washing can damage shingles and void warranty coverage on Timberline, Camelot, and Glenwood product lines. CertainTeed publishes similar guidance through its Maintenance and Care document set, requiring low-pressure cleaning and warning against high-pressure equipment. CertainTeed's Landmark and Presidential warranties both reference proper maintenance as a coverage condition.

Owens Corning includes a roof-cleaning advisory in its TruDefinition Duration warranty documentation that specifies low-pressure cleaning methods only, with a warning that high-pressure cleaning can disturb the algae-resistant granules incorporated into newer Duration product lines and may void the StreakGuard algae warranty. Atlas, manufacturer of Pinnacle Pristine and StormMaster lines, publishes a similar maintenance guide requiring low-pressure cleaning. The recurring industry standard across all four manufacturers is: cleaning solution does the work, water force does not. The water exists to carry the cleaning agent to the surface and rinse the dissolved organic material away — not to physically blast contamination from the shingle.

Why High Pressure Damages Shingles

Asphalt shingles are not solid. They consist of a fiberglass mat saturated with asphalt, topped with a layer of embedded ceramic granules and an adhesive tar strip on the underside. The granules serve three purposes: they protect the asphalt from UV degradation (the asphalt would harden and crack within years without them), they provide color, and they add fire resistance. The granules are pressed into the asphalt during manufacturing but are not chemically bonded — they are mechanically held in place by the asphalt's tackiness and the friction of adjacent granules.

When a 2,500–4,000 PSI water stream hits a shingle surface, it physically dislodges granules in the impact zone. Some come off immediately; others are loosened and wash off in the next rainfall. The shingle below the impact zone is now partially exposed asphalt with reduced UV protection, accelerating degradation over the following years. High pressure can also lift shingle edges from the adhesive tar strip below, creating channels for wind-driven rain to penetrate the underlayment and decking. In extreme cases, the pressure can crack older, brittle shingles outright. The damage from a single high-pressure cleaning is rarely visible the day of service — it shows up 2–5 years later as accelerated granule loss, premature shingle curl, and roof failure 5–10 years before the manufacturer's rated service life. How to Verify Your Specific Warranty

Every major shingle manufacturer maintains digital warranty documentation accessible through their website. To find your specific warranty terms, you'll need (1) the manufacturer name, (2) the product line (e.g., GAF Timberline HDZ, CertainTeed Landmark, Owens Corning TruDefinition Duration), and (3) ideally the installation date. The product line is typically listed on your closing documents from the home purchase, on the original roofing invoice, or by checking with the roofing contractor who installed it. If those records are missing, a roofing inspector can usually identify the shingle line from a few minutes on the roof.

Once you have the product line, visit the manufacturer's website (gaf.com, certainteed.com, owenscorning.com, atlasroofing.com) and search for "warranty," "limited warranty," or "maintenance and care guide" for your specific product. Within those documents, look for the terms "cleaning," "maintenance," "pressure," and "PSI." All four major manufacturers include these terms in their warranty documentation. If the warranty document is unavailable or you cannot determine your product line, contact the manufacturer's customer service department directly — most will respond to homeowner cleaning-method questions within 1–2 business days. Keep a copy of any written response in your home maintenance records.

What Soft Washing Does Differently

Soft washing is the only roof cleaning methodology that fully complies with major shingle manufacturer warranty requirements. The process uses water at under 100 PSI — less than a standard garden hose with a spray nozzle — combined with a calibrated sodium hypochlorite cleaning solution that does the actual work. The chemistry kills the Gloeocapsa magma cyanobacteria, lichen, and mildew at the cellular level. The water simply rinses the dissolved organic material away after the chemistry has done its job.

Because the pressure is so low, soft washing does not dislodge granules, does not lift shingle edges, does not break the adhesive tar strip, and does not force water into the underlayment. The cleaning solution itself is a low-concentration variant (1–3% sodium hypochlorite plus a surfactant) of household-bleach chemistry that's been used in roof cleaning since the 1990s — it has a well-documented safety profile for shingle surfaces. The methodology is taught by the UAMCC (United Association of Mobile Contract Cleaners) and is the basis of every reputable professional roof cleaning company's training program. Our roof soft washing service stays well within the under-100 PSI threshold required by GAF, CertainTeed, Owens Corning, and Atlas, and we can provide a written statement of methodology for your warranty records on request.

Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Roof Cleaner

Before signing a contract or scheduling roof cleaning service, ask any prospective provider the following five questions. Their answers reveal almost everything you need to know about the methodology they use and the risk you'd be taking.

1. What PSI will you use on my roof? The correct answer is "under 100 PSI" or "soft wash only." Any answer above that or vague responses like "we use the right pressure" should be a disqualifier. 2. Do you carry liability insurance, and can you provide a current Certificate of Insurance? Reputable companies carry at least $1M general liability and can produce a current COI within 24 hours. 3. Will your cleaning method comply with my shingle manufacturer's warranty requirements? A competent provider will know the answer is yes (because they use soft washing) or will pause and want to verify before answering. An incompetent provider will either dismiss the question or claim it doesn't matter. 4. Can you provide a written satisfaction guarantee? The answer should be yes, with specific terms. 5. Do you walk on the roof during cleaning, or do you clean from ground level? Best practice is to clean from ground level using extension wands and telescoping poles — walking on shingles risks damage, particularly on hot Florida days when asphalt is soft. If a provider's answers to these questions are evasive or contradictory, find a different provider. The roof is the most expensive single component of your home's exterior, and the cleaning method choice has direct, multi-thousand-dollar warranty implications.
