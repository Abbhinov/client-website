Lanai & Screen Enclosure Cleaning

Clean Slate Pressure Washing provides professional lanai and screen enclosure cleaning for homes throughout Brandon, Riverview, Valrico, Lithia, FishHawk, and the Tampa Bay region. Florida's pool cage enclosures accumulate a persistent film of pollen, mildew, oxidation, and water mineral deposits that block natural light and trap humidity inside the lanai space. Our soft wash process cleans all three components of the enclosure in a single visit: the aluminum frame, the screen panels, and the floor surface (concrete, pavers, or travertine). We use low-pressure soft washing at under 200 PSI on screens and frames to prevent tearing — our no-torn-screens guarantee means if we damage a screen panel during cleaning, we replace it at our cost. The floor surface is cleaned at the appropriate pressure for its material type.

The Lanai That Used to Feel Like a Room

When the cage was new, the late afternoon light came through clean — the pool turned blue, the screens looked like windows, the aluminum framing was bright. Now it all reads gray. There's a film on every screen panel that traps the pollen and won't rinse off. The frame bars near the corners have darkened from oxidation. The floor inside the cage has gone from white concrete to a mottled green-and-black map of mildew, particularly along the perimeter where rain runs down the screens. The whole space feels closed-in instead of open, even though nothing has physically changed.

Trying to fix this with a regular pressure washer is how torn screens happen. Screen mesh is thin, the spline that holds it can be 10+ years old, and ordinary pressure-washer tips deliver far too much force at close range. The correct fix is soft washing at under 200 PSI with a cleaning solution that dissolves the film at the molecular level — applied from both sides of each panel, rinsed gently, then the floor cleaned at proper concrete or paver pressure. Done right, the cage looks new from the curb and from the pool.

How We Do It

Our 5-Step Lanai Cleaning Process

  1. Enclosure assessment

    We inspect all screen panels for pre-existing tears, loose spline, or weakened screens. Any pre-existing damage is documented and discussed with the homeowner before cleaning begins.

  2. Interior floor pre-treatment

    The concrete or paver floor inside the enclosure receives a cleaning solution application to break down organic growth, especially in shaded corners and along the cage perimeter.

  3. Screen and frame soft wash

    Our low-pressure soft wash system (under 200 PSI) applies cleaning solution to screens and aluminum frames from inside and outside the enclosure, dissolving pollen film, mildew, and oxidation.

  4. Gentle rinse

    All screens and frames are rinsed with low-pressure water. We rinse from top to bottom, inside to outside, ensuring complete removal of all cleaning agents.

  5. Floor surface cleaning

    The enclosure floor is pressure cleaned at the appropriate level for its material. Pool furniture is moved as needed and replaced after cleaning.
