Rust & Stain Removal for Concrete & Pavers

Clean Slate Pressure Washing provides specialized rust and stain removal services for concrete, pavers, and natural stone surfaces throughout Brandon, Riverview, Valrico, and the Tampa Bay region. Standard pressure washing cannot remove many common Florida stains — rust from irrigation well water, orange marks from fertilizer granules, battery acid drips on garage floors, tannin stains from oak leaves and acorns, and black marks from decomposing landscape mulch all require specific chemical treatments matched to the stain chemistry. Our technicians identify the stain type, select the appropriate removal agent (oxalic acid for rust and tannins, sodium hydroxide for organic stains, hydrofluoric acid compounds for severe mineral deposits), apply the treatment, and pressure clean the area after the chemistry has done its work. Most stains require 1–2 treatment cycles for complete removal.

The Stains That Don't Come Out With Pressure

You've already pressure washed the driveway. The general grime is gone. But the bright orange line under the irrigation head is still there. The half-circle of brown where the fertilizer spreader sat is still there. The two dark drip rings on the garage floor from when the car battery leaked — still there. These are not dirt. They are chemical bonds that have penetrated into the concrete's pore structure, and water force alone cannot lift them out no matter how many passes you make.

Each stain type has its own chemistry, and the correct removal agent has to match. Iron-based stains (irrigation well rust, fertilizer rust) come out with oxalic acid that converts the iron oxide back into a water-soluble form. Tannins from oak and decomposing mulch respond to the same chemistry. Battery acid is an etching event — the surface texture may be permanently altered, but the discoloration can usually be removed and the area blended. Natural stone needs pH-specific gentler chemistry to avoid etching the surface itself. The right fix is identification first, test spot second, full treatment third — and a second cycle if needed at no extra charge.

How We Do It

Our 5-Step Rust & Stain Removal Process

  1. Stain identification

    We determine the stain type (rust, tannin, battery acid, fertilizer, organic, mineral) and the surface material (concrete, pavers, travertine, flagstone) to select the correct chemistry.

  2. Test spot

    A small inconspicuous area is treated first to verify the selected chemistry reacts correctly with both the stain and the surface material without discoloration or etching.

  3. Treatment application

    The matched removal agent is applied to the stained area and allowed to dwell for the prescribed time. Rust stains require 15–30 minutes; organic stains require 10–20 minutes.

  4. Agitation and rinse

    Stubborn stains receive light mechanical agitation with a stiff brush before rinsing. The treatment area is pressure cleaned to remove all chemistry and dissolved stain material.

  5. Second cycle (if needed)

    Deeply penetrated stains may require a second application. We assess results after the first cycle dries (15–20 minutes) and retreat as necessary at no additional charge within the same visit.
