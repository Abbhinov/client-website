Why Murray Hill Homes Have Specific HVAC Needs

1940s–1960s Bungalows and Cottages — Murray Hill’s housing stock is dominated by small bungalows and cottages built in the 1940s through 1960s, typically 900–1,400 square feet on compact lots. These homes were designed for an era before central air conditioning was standard in Florida. Original construction often includes concrete block walls, single-pane jalousie or awning windows, minimal insulation, and no dedicated mechanical closet for HVAC equipment. Retrofitting modern AC into these homes requires creative air handler placement, compact ductwork design, and system sizing that accounts for the higher heat gain from poor insulation and single-pane glass. Cool & Cozy has extensive experience with this exact housing type across Jacksonville’s older Westside neighborhoods.

Revitalization Bringing New Owners to Old Homes — Murray Hill’s arts district revival along Edgewood Avenue has attracted a wave of new homebuyers purchasing and renovating older homes. These buyers often discover HVAC challenges during renovation: non-existent ductwork, undersized electrical panels (60–100 amp services common in this era), and previous HVAC installations that were done cheaply rather than correctly. Cool & Cozy works with renovation-stage homeowners to design HVAC systems that integrate properly with the renovation plan rather than being an afterthought bolted on at the end.

Affordable Housing and Rental Market — Murray Hill’s affordability makes it a significant rental market, with single-family rentals, duplexes, and small multi-family properties. Property managers and landlords in Murray Hill need a contractor who understands older, quirkier systems and can maintain them reliably at a price point that works within the neighborhood’s rental economics. Cool & Cozy’s transparent per-unit pricing and bilingual tenant communication serve Murray Hill’s diverse renter population effectively.

Compact Lots and Access Challenges — Murray Hill’s narrow lots and close house spacing create access challenges for HVAC service: tight side yards limit equipment delivery, outdoor units sit close to property lines (neighbor noise sensitivity), and attic access in single-story bungalows often requires maneuvering through cramped ceiling hatches. Cool & Cozy’s technicians are experienced working in these constrained spaces and plan equipment placement to balance performance, accessibility for future service, and neighbor considerations.

FAQ 1
Q: Can Cool & Cozy install central AC in my Murray Hill bungalow that doesn’t have ductwork?
A: Yes. Many Murray Hill bungalows were built before central air was standard and may have window units or no cooling at all. Cool & Cozy offers two solutions: a conventional central system with compact ductwork designed for smaller homes (if attic or crawlspace access allows), or a ductless mini-split system that cools individual rooms without any ductwork. Mini-splits are often the best fit for Murray Hill’s compact floor plans and limited mechanical space. Call (904) 555-0199 for a free consultation.

FAQ 2
Q: I’m renovating a Murray Hill home. When should I plan the HVAC?
A: Plan HVAC early in the renovation — ideally during the design phase before walls are closed up. This allows ductwork to be routed through wall cavities, electrical upgrades to be coordinated with the HVAC load, and equipment placement to be integrated with the floor plan rather than awkwardly retrofitted afterward. Cool & Cozy works with renovation-stage homeowners and their contractors to design systems that fit the renovation plan. Call (904) 555-0199 to discuss your project before construction begins.
