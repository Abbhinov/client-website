1. How to Use This Guide
This guide defines the Emergency AC Repair page at /services/emergency-ac-repair. This is the most urgency-driven page on the entire site. The visitor landing here has a non-functional AC system during dangerous heat conditions and is scanning for a phone number, not reading marketing copy. Every design decision, content block, and layout choice must serve one objective: get the visitor to call (904) 555-0199 within 10 seconds of landing.
The page still carries proper SEO structure, AEO content, and schema because it needs to RANK for emergency queries to be useful. But the content hierarchy is inverted compared to other service pages: the phone number comes first, the educational content supports the call decision, and nothing gets between the visitor and the action.
1.1 Design Philosophy: Phone-First
The phone number appears in the emergency banner, the header, the hero (as the ONLY CTA — no secondary form button), and is repeated after every content section.
Content sections are shorter than other service pages. An emergency visitor scrolls fast and scans, not reads.
The page uses more Coral (#E8603C) and higher-contrast elements than other pages to communicate urgency visually without using alarm-red.
The emergency banner on this page should ALWAYS show the summer/emergency variant regardless of season.
The sticky mobile CTA bar shows ONLY the phone call button on this page (not the dual call/estimate split).

5.4 What to Do While You Wait for Emergency AC Repair
Overview
Practical, actionable advice for the homeowner who just called and is waiting for the technician. This section serves two purposes: (1) genuinely helpful content that builds trust, and (2) targets the AEO query “what to do when AC breaks in Florida” / “AC emergency what to do.”

H2
While You Wait: 6 Steps to Stay Safe and Protect Your System

#
Step
1
Turn the system OFF at the thermostat. If you hear unusual noises, smell burning, or see ice forming, continuing to run the system can cause additional damage. Switching to “Off” or “Fan Only” prevents compressor damage.
2
Check your circuit breaker panel. A tripped breaker is one of the most common causes of sudden AC failure. If the HVAC breaker is tripped, reset it once. If it trips again immediately, leave it off — this indicates an electrical fault that needs professional diagnosis.
3
Check your thermostat. Verify it is set to “Cool” (not “Heat” or “Off”), the temperature is set below the current room temperature, and the batteries are not dead (if it uses batteries). A blank thermostat screen often just needs fresh batteries.
4
Open interior doors and any windows that catch a breeze. Use portable fans to circulate air. Close blinds and curtains on sun-facing windows to reduce heat gain. Move to the lowest level of your home (heat rises).
5
Stay hydrated. In Jacksonville’s humidity, dehydration happens faster than you expect. Drink water, avoid alcohol and caffeine, and keep pets’ water bowls full. If elderly family members or infants are present and indoor temperatures exceed 85°F, consider relocating them temporarily.
6
Clear the area around your outdoor unit. Remove any debris, vegetation, or objects within 2 feet of the condenser. This gives the technician immediate access on arrival and may even resolve airflow-related issues.

Our technician is on the way. Call (904) 555-0199 if anything changes.
Steps 1–3 address the most common user-resolvable causes of AC failure. A meaningful percentage of “emergency” calls are tripped breakers or dead thermostat batteries. Providing this advice up front is honest and builds trust — the customer remembers that Cool & Cozy tried to help them fix it themselves first.
