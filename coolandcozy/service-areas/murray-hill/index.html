Why Murray Hill Homes Have Specific HVAC Needs

Four local differentiators rooted in Murray Hill's housing stock, geography, and demographics — not a generic Jacksonville template.

1940s–1960s Bungalows and Cottages

Murray Hill's housing stock is dominated by small bungalows and cottages built in the 1940s through 1960s, typically 900–1,400 square feet on compact lots. These homes were designed for an era before central air conditioning was standard in Florida. Original construction often includes concrete block walls, single-pane jalousie or awning windows, minimal insulation, and no dedicated mechanical closet for HVAC equipment. Retrofitting modern AC into these homes requires creative air handler placement, compact ductwork design, and system sizing that accounts for the higher heat gain from poor insulation and single-pane glass. Cool & Cozy has extensive experience with this exact housing type across Jacksonville's older Westside neighborhoods.

Revitalization Bringing New Owners to Old Homes

Murray Hill's arts district revival along Edgewood Avenue has attracted a wave of new homebuyers purchasing and renovating older homes. These buyers often discover HVAC challenges during renovation: non-existent ductwork, undersized electrical panels (60–100 amp services common in this era), and previous HVAC installations that were done cheaply rather than correctly. Cool & Cozy works with renovation-stage homeowners to design HVAC systems that integrate properly with the renovation plan rather than being an afterthought bolted on at the end.

Compact Lots and Access Challenges

Murray Hill's narrow lots and close house spacing create access challenges for HVAC service: tight side yards limit equipment delivery, outdoor units sit close to property lines (neighbor noise sensitivity), and attic access in single-story bungalows often requires maneuvering through cramped ceiling hatches. Cool & Cozy's technicians are experienced working in these constrained spaces and plan equipment placement to balance performance, accessibility for future service, and neighbor considerations.
