How Do You Know If Your Ducts Are the Problem?

Most homeowners don't realize their ducts are leaking — they just notice the symptoms. Here's how duct problems show up in Jacksonville homes.

Rooms that never cool evenly

If some rooms reach the thermostat's set temperature while others stay warm, the ductwork serving those rooms may be leaking, disconnected, or undersized. This is especially common in rooms at the end of long duct runs or rooms served by flexible duct that has been crushed or kinked.

Unusually high energy bills

When 20–30% of your cooled air leaks into the attic or crawlspace, your AC runs longer and harder to compensate. On a $200/month summer JEA bill, that's $40–$60/month you're paying to cool your attic instead of your living room.

Excessive dust in the house

Leaky return ducts pull unconditioned attic or crawlspace air — along with dust, insulation fibers, and allergens — into your living space. If you're dusting constantly and changing filters more than monthly, your ducts may be the source.

Visible duct damage

If you can see your ductwork in the attic or crawlspace and notice disconnected joints, crushed flex duct, sagging runs, torn insulation, or rodent chew marks, those are direct air leaks that need repair.

Whistling, rattling, or popping noises from vents

These sounds often indicate a partially blocked or kinked duct, a loose connection vibrating against framing, or sheet metal expanding and contracting from temperature changes.

Musty or stale odors from vents

In Jacksonville's humidity, condensation inside poorly insulated ducts creates conditions for mold and mildew growth. If turning on the AC produces a musty smell, the ductwork may need inspection and remediation.

Ductwork Services for Jacksonville Homes and Properties

Four service categories covering everything from quick leak fixes to complete duct system replacement.

Service 1

Duct Inspection & Leak Testing

We perform a visual and pressure-based inspection of your entire duct system. This includes accessing attic and crawlspace runs, checking every connection point, measuring airflow at each supply vent, and identifying leaks, disconnections, and insulation failures. You receive a written report with findings, photos, and recommendations — before any work is proposed.

Typical scenario: Homeowner suspects duct issues but isn't sure; new-to-homeowner wanting baseline assessment; pre-installation duct condition check.

Service 2

Duct Sealing & Repair

We seal leaks at connections, joints, and boot-to-register transitions using mastic sealant and metal-backed tape (UL-listed, not cloth "duct tape" which fails in attic heat). Disconnected sections are reconnected and secured with mechanical fasteners. Crushed or kinked flex duct is repositioned and supported properly. Small holes and tears are patched; larger damage may require section replacement.

Typical scenario: Uneven cooling, high energy bills, visible duct damage, post-repair airflow improvement.

Service 3

Duct Replacement

When ductwork is too deteriorated for repair — common in Jacksonville homes built before 1990 with original flex duct that has become brittle, separated from boots, or lost its insulation R-value — we replace duct runs with properly sized, insulated, and sealed new ductwork. Replacement may involve individual runs or the complete duct system depending on overall condition.

Typical scenario: 40–60 year old ductwork in pre-1990 homes, rodent-damaged runs, ductwork failing during AC installation.

Service 4

Duct Modification & New Runs

When you add a room, convert a garage, or reconfigure living space, the existing duct system may not reach the new area. We design and install new duct runs sized to your AC system's capacity, ensuring the added space receives adequate airflow without starving existing rooms. We also modify ductwork during new AC installations when the replacement system has different airflow requirements than the original.

Typical scenario: Room additions, garage conversions, layout changes, new AC system with different airflow specs.

Why Jacksonville's Climate Makes Your Ductwork a Critical Factor

Ductwork problems are worse here than in cooler climates. Florida's heat, humidity, aging housing stock, and year-round pest activity all compound the energy waste of even small duct leaks.

The Numbers

  • 20–30% of cooled air typically lost to leaks (U.S. DOE)
  • Attic temps exceed 140°F in Jacksonville summers
  • 2,500+ AC runtime hours per year in our climate
  • 40–60 year old flex duct still in many pre-1990 homes

Attic temperatures exceed 140°F in summer

Most Jacksonville homes route ductwork through unconditioned attic space. When your attic hits 140°F and your supply air is 55°F, the temperature difference across the duct wall is 85 degrees. Poorly insulated or uninsulated ducts absorb that heat, delivering warm air to rooms instead of cold. Even well-insulated ducts lose efficiency in this extreme gradient.

Humidity creates condensation inside poorly insulated ducts

When cold supply air moves through hot, humid attic space in ducts with inadequate insulation, condensation forms on the duct exterior — or worse, inside the duct. This moisture promotes mold growth, degrades insulation, and can drip onto ceilings below, causing water stains and structural damage.

Jacksonville's older housing stock has aging ductwork

Neighborhoods like Arlington, Southside, San Jose, and older sections of Mandarin contain significant numbers of homes built in the 1960s through 1980s. The original flex duct in these homes is now 40–60 years old. Flex duct was not designed for that lifespan — the inner liner separates, insulation compresses and loses R-value, and connections loosen over decades of thermal cycling.

Rodent and pest damage is common in crawlspaces

Jacksonville's warm climate supports year-round rodent and insect activity. Rats, mice, and squirrels commonly nest in ductwork running through crawlspaces and attics, chewing holes in flex duct and contaminating the air supply with droppings and nesting material.

Your AC runs 2,500+ hours per year through these ducts

Every hour of operation with leaking ducts compounds the energy waste. Over a cooling season, a duct system leaking 25% of its air costs the equivalent of running your AC for an extra 625 hours — roughly three months of additional runtime that you're paying JEA for without receiving any cooling benefit.

Ductwork repair costs vary widely depending on the scope. Sealing accessible leaks at connections and joints typically costs $300 to $700. Repairing or replacing individual damaged duct runs ranges from $500 to $1,500 per run depending on location and accessibility. Full duct system replacement for a typical Jacksonville home ranges from $2,000 to $5,000+. Cool & Cozy provides a free inspection with a written report and quote before any work begins.

Cool & Cozy performs pressure testing and visual inspection to identify and quantify duct leaks. In many cases, we can show you the leaks directly during the inspection.

Cool & Cozy focuses on ductwork repair, sealing, and replacement rather than standalone duct cleaning. Our perspective, based on EPA guidance, is that duct cleaning alone is rarely effective if the underlying leaks and damage are not addressed first. Cleaning ducts that are still leaking means you're cleaning contamination that will return immediately. We do clean accessible duct surfaces as part of repair and replacement projects, and we can recommend reputable duct cleaning specialists if cleaning is appropriate for your situation after the duct system is sealed.

Sealing and minor repairs are typically completed in half a day (3 to 4 hours). Replacing individual duct runs takes a full day. Complete duct system replacement for a typical Jacksonville home takes 1 to 2 days depending on the size of the home and accessibility of the duct routing. We provide a specific timeline during your free inspection based on the scope of work your system needs.
