1. Blog Strategy Overview
1.1 Why a Blog Matters for Easy Clean Service
Easy Clean’s service pages target transactional keywords (“deep cleaning Tampa”) and the neighborhood pages target local keywords (“house cleaning Westchase”). But above these in the search funnel sits a massive layer of informational queries – people asking questions, researching cleaning topics, and comparing options before they are ready to hire. These searchers are future customers, and right now Easy Clean has zero content to capture them.
A strategic blog solves four problems simultaneously. First, it captures informational search traffic that no other page on the site can rank for – queries like “how much does house cleaning cost in Tampa,” “how to remove hard water stains,” and “deep cleaning vs regular cleaning.” Second, it builds topical authority that strengthens the rankings of every service and neighborhood page by signaling to Google that Easy Clean is a genuine expert on residential cleaning, not just a thin local business listing. Third, it provides source material for AI answer engines – Perplexity, ChatGPT search, and Google AI Overviews draw heavily from well-structured informational content when assembling answers about local services. Fourth, it creates shareable content for social media that drives brand awareness and referral traffic once Easy Clean’s social accounts are established.
* Traffic: Capture 500+ monthly organic visits from informational keywords within 6 months of consistent publishing.
* Authority: Build topical authority for “house cleaning Tampa” semantic cluster, lifting all service and neighborhood page rankings.
* GEO/AEO: Produce content structured for AI answer engine extraction – clear questions, definitive answers, Tampa-specific data points.
* Conversion: Every blog post funnels readers to a relevant service page or estimate form via strategic internal links and CTAs.
* Social: Provide a content library for Facebook and Instagram posts once social accounts are established.
1.3 Publishing Cadence
Recommended: 2 posts per month for the first 90 days (6 posts total), then increase to 4 per month as the blog gains traction.
Minimum viable: 1 post per month. Anything less than this will not generate meaningful organic results.
Post length: 1,200–2,000 words per post. Shorter posts lack the depth to rank; longer posts provide diminishing returns for this business type.

3. Content Pillar Framework
Every blog post should map to one of four content pillars. Each pillar serves a different strategic purpose and targets a different segment of the search funnel.
Pillar 1: Cost & Pricing Guides
  FUNNEL STAGE: Bottom – High purchase intent  
These posts target the highest-volume informational keywords in the cleaning space. Searchers asking about cost are typically days or weeks away from hiring someone. Every cost guide should include Tampa-specific pricing data, a comparison table, and a strong CTA to the relevant service page estimate form.
Link strategy: Each cost post links to its corresponding service page (e.g., deep cleaning cost post → /services/deep-cleaning-tampa/).
Pillar 2: Service Comparisons & Decision Guides
  FUNNEL STAGE: Middle – Researching options  
These posts help readers understand which cleaning service fits their situation. They naturally create internal links to multiple service pages per post. They are also the highest-performing content type for AI answer engines because they provide structured, definitive comparisons.
Link strategy: Each comparison post links to 2–4 service pages. The /services/ overview page is also a natural link target.
Pillar 3: Tampa Home Care & Maintenance
  FUNNEL STAGE: Top – Awareness and authority building  
These posts address the specific cleaning challenges of living in Tampa and Florida – humidity, mold, hard water stains, pollen, sandy floors, hurricane prep. They build local topical authority and position Easy Clean as the Tampa cleaning expert. They also serve as excellent social media content.
Link strategy: Link to neighborhood pages where relevant (e.g., “old Carrollwood terrazzo floors” links to the Carrollwood page) and to the relevant service page.
Pillar 4: Seasonal & Event-Driven Content
  FUNNEL STAGE: Top/Middle – Timely traffic spikes  
These posts capture predictable seasonal search surges: spring cleaning, holiday prep, back-to-school, hurricane season, snowbird arrival, end-of-lease moves. They should be published 4–6 weeks before the seasonal peak to allow time for Google to index and rank them.
Link strategy: Seasonal posts link to multiple service pages and the /services/ overview. Holiday posts link to deep cleaning; moving posts link to move-in/out cleaning.

6. Blog Post Structure Template
1. Title Tag: Primary keyword + Tampa/FL geo-modifier + year if applicable. Under 60 characters.
2. Meta Description: Summarize the post’s value prop + include a CTA + phone number. 150–155 characters.
3. H1 Heading: Same as or very close to the title tag. Only one H1 per post.
4. Intro paragraph (100–150 words): State the question the post answers, establish Tampa relevance, and tell the reader what they will learn. Include primary keyword in first sentence.
5. Body sections (H2 headings): 3–6 H2 sections, each targeting a secondary keyword or subtopic. Use H3 for subsections within.
6. Tables and lists: Include at least one comparison table or data table per post. These are the elements AI answer engines are most likely to extract.
7. Images: 1 featured image (1200x630px for social sharing) + 2–3 in-body images with keyword-rich alt text.
8. Internal links: Minimum 2 service page links + 1 neighborhood page link (when relevant) + 1 link to another blog post.
9. FAQ section (bottom): 3–5 questions in Q&A format with FAQPage schema markup. Target long-tail keyword variants and featured snippet opportunities.
10. CTA section (end): Full-width CTA banner: “Ready to [relevant action]? Call (813) 534-9216 or get your free estimate.” Link to /contact/ or the relevant service page.
11. Author byline: “By Kessia Marquez, Owner of Easy Clean Service” with link to /about/. This establishes E-E-A-T authorship.
12. Schema: Article schema with author, datePublished, dateModified, publisher, and image. FAQPage schema for the FAQ section.
7. GEO/AEO Optimization for Blog Content
Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) and Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) ensure that blog content is structured for extraction by AI systems like Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT search, and voice assistants. Here are the specific guidelines for every blog post:
* Lead with the answer: The first 2–3 sentences of every post should directly and definitively answer the primary question. AI systems extract from the top of the content. Do not bury the answer below 3 paragraphs of context.
* Use question-format H2 headings: “How much does deep cleaning cost in Tampa?” is better than “Deep Cleaning Pricing” because it mirrors how users query AI systems.
* Include definitive numbers: AI systems strongly prefer content with specific data points: “$200–$450 for a 3-bedroom Tampa home” is extractable; “prices vary” is not.
* Use comparison tables: Tables are the single most AI-extractable content format. Every post should include at least one data table.
* Write FAQ sections in pure Q&A format: Question as H3, answer as the immediately following paragraph. Keep answers concise (2–4 sentences) for featured snippet capture.
* Name the city in the first 100 words: Every post should mention Tampa, FL within the first two sentences.
* Reference specific neighborhoods: Mention at least 2–3 Tampa neighborhoods by name in the body copy with links to their pages.
* Cite Florida-specific data: Reference Florida’s humidity levels, state-specific laws (e.g., security deposit statutes), and local housing characteristics.
* Include the business name and phone number: At least once in the body and in the CTA. AI systems associate content with specific businesses when they are named.
* Article schema: @type Article with headline, author (Kessia Marquez), datePublished, dateModified, publisher (Easy Clean Service), image.
* FAQPage schema: For the bottom-of-post FAQ section. Must mirror visible content exactly.
* BreadcrumbList: Home > Blog > [Post Title]
