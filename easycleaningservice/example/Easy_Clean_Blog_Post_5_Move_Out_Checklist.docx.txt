H1: The Ultimate Move-Out Cleaning Checklist for Tampa Renters
  POST CONTENT BEGINS  
Introduction
Moving out of a rental in Tampa means more than loading boxes into a truck. Your lease almost certainly requires you to return the property in clean, move-in-ready condition – and failing to meet that standard can cost you hundreds of dollars from your security deposit. In Florida, landlords have 15 to 60 days after you vacate to either return your full deposit or send you an itemized list of deductions, and cleaning charges are consistently among the most common reasons Tampa tenants lose part or all of their deposit money.
This checklist covers every area that Tampa landlords and property management companies typically inspect during a move-out walkthrough. Use it as your room-by-room guide whether you are doing the cleaning yourself or hiring a professional to handle it. H2: Florida Security Deposit Law – What Tampa Renters Need to Know
  GEO/AEO TARGET: Florida-specific legal context for AI extraction  
* Return timeline: Under Florida Statute 83.49, your landlord has 15 days to return your full deposit if they have no claim against it. If they intend to make deductions, they must notify you in writing within 30 days of your move-out with an itemized list of damages and amounts. If they fail to send this notice within 30 days, they may forfeit their right to make any claim against your deposit.
* Cleaning is a common deduction: Landlords can legally deduct for cleaning if the property is not returned in the condition it was in at move-in (minus normal wear and tear). “Normal wear and tear” covers things like minor scuff marks on walls, faded paint, and carpet wear in high-traffic paths. It does not cover greasy stovetops, mildewed shower tile, dirty oven interiors, or stained toilet bowls – those are cleaning deficiencies.
* The cost gap: Professional move-out cleaning for a typical Tampa apartment costs $250 to $400. If your landlord hires their own cleaning crew and bills you, the charge is often $400 to $800 or more – because they have no incentive to shop for a competitive rate when it is coming out of your deposit. Hiring your own cleaner before the walkthrough is almost always cheaper than letting the landlord handle it and deducting the cost.
* Document everything: Take timestamped photos of every room after cleaning and before handing over the keys. If there is a dispute about cleaning, photographic evidence of the property’s condition at move-out is your strongest protection.
NOTE: This section is not legal advice. It summarizes publicly available Florida statute provisions for general informational purposes. Readers with specific legal questions should consult a Florida landlord-tenant attorney.

H2: Kitchen Move-Out Cleaning Checklist
* Clean inside the oven including racks, walls, door glass, and gasket
* Clean inside the microwave including turntable, ceiling, and door seal
* Clean inside the refrigerator including all shelves, drawers, door bins, and rubber door seals
* Clean inside the dishwasher and wipe the door edges and front panel
* Degrease the stovetop, burner grates or glass cooktop surface, and range hood filter
* Sanitize all countertops and backsplash areas
* Scrub the sink basin, polish the faucet, and clean the disposal splash guard
* Wipe the inside and outside of every cabinet and drawer (remove shelf liner if required by lease)
* Clean the exterior of all appliances including the sides of the refrigerator
* Wipe all light switch plates, outlet covers, and door handles
* Clean the light fixture or ceiling fan
* Sweep and mop the floor including behind where the refrigerator and stove were positioned
* Clean the interior of the trash can area or pull-out trash cabinet
The kitchen is typically the most scrutinized room during a move-out inspection. Landlords know that oven interiors, refrigerator shelves, and range hood grease are the items renters are most likely to skip. Addressing every item on this list removes the most common kitchen-related deductions.
* Scrub and disinfect the toilet completely: bowl interior, exterior, base, hinges, tank exterior, and behind the tank
* Deep-scrub the bathtub or shower including walls, floor, door or curtain rod, and tile grout
* Remove hard water stains and soap scum from all glass, tile, and chrome surfaces
* Clean and polish the sink basin, faucet, and all hardware (towel bars, toilet paper holder, hooks)
* Clean the mirror and medicine cabinet exterior (and interior if accessible)
* Wipe inside all vanity cabinets and drawers
* Clean the exhaust fan vent cover (remove and wash if possible)
* Wipe all light switch plates and outlet covers
* Scrub the floor on hands and knees including behind the toilet, along edges, and in corners
* Clean shower door tracks if applicable (vacuum debris first, then scrub)
* Remove and dispose of any old shower curtain or liner
* Check caulk around the tub and shower – note any mold staining for the walkthrough
Tampa’s humidity makes bathrooms the hardest room to get deposit-ready. Mildew on grout, hard water stains on glass shower doors, and soap scum buildup are the most common bathroom deductions in Tampa move-out inspections. If your grout is heavily stained despite scrubbing, professional treatment may be the only way to restore it before inspection.

H2: Additional Areas Landlords Check
* Laundry area: Wipe the washer/dryer connections (if units removed), clean the dryer vent area, sweep and mop the laundry room floor.
* Garage (if applicable): Sweep the garage floor, remove any oil stains with a degreaser, clear all personal items from shelves and storage areas.
* Patio / balcony / lanai: Sweep the floor, clean the railing, remove any personal items. In Tampa, lanai screens should be checked for tears – note any damage for the walkthrough.
* AC filter: Replace the AC filter with a new one. This is a small cost ($5–15) that landlords almost always check and frequently deduct for. Buy the correct size and install it before the walkthrough.
* Smoke and CO detectors: Replace batteries in all smoke and carbon monoxide detectors. Some Tampa leases require this at move-out.
* Light bulbs: Replace any burned-out light bulbs. Some leases specify that all bulbs must be working at move-out.
* Keys and access devices: Return all keys, garage door openers, mailbox keys, pool fobs, and gate remotes. Missing keys often result in rekeying charges of $50–$150 per lock.

H2: When to Schedule Your Move-Out Cleaning
1. 2 weeks before move-out: Book your professional cleaning (or block time on your calendar for DIY). Cleaning companies get booked quickly at the end of each month when most leases expire.
2. Moving day or day before: Remove all personal belongings and furniture from the property. The more empty the home is, the more thorough the cleaning can be.
3. Day after move-out (ideal): Schedule the professional cleaning for the day after your movers leave. This gives the team access to every surface, corner, and closet without working around boxes and furniture.
4. After cleaning: Walk through the property with your phone camera. Photograph every room, every appliance interior, and every bathroom. Timestamp the photos. These are your evidence if the landlord disputes the cleaning condition.
5. Key handoff: Return all keys and access devices to the landlord or property manager. Request written confirmation of receipt.

Q1: What do landlords check during a move-out inspection in Tampa?
Tampa landlords and property managers typically inspect inside all kitchen appliances (oven, refrigerator, microwave, dishwasher), bathroom grout and fixtures, inside all cabinets and closets, baseboards, window tracks and blinds, light fixtures, flooring condition, walls for damage beyond normal wear and tear, and the overall cleanliness of every room. The kitchen and bathrooms receive the most scrutiny.
Q2: How much does move-out cleaning cost in Tampa?
Professional move-out cleaning in Tampa typically ranges from $180 to $650 depending on the size and condition of the property. A standard 2-bedroom, 2-bathroom apartment usually falls between $250 and $380. This is almost always less than what a landlord would deduct from your security deposit if they hire their own cleaning crew. Call (813) 534-9216 for a free quote based on your specific property.
Q3: How far in advance should I book move-out cleaning?
We recommend booking one to two weeks before your move-out date. End-of-month dates fill up fastest because that is when most Tampa leases expire. If you are on short notice, call us – we can often accommodate requests within 24 to 48 hours depending on our schedule.
Q4: Can I use this checklist for move-in cleaning too?
Yes, the room-by-room cleaning tasks are nearly identical for move-in and move-out. The main difference is the mindset: move-out cleaning focuses on returning the property to landlord inspection standards, while move-in cleaning focuses on sanitizing every surface for your family’s comfort and health. Easy Clean offers both services and can schedule them on the same day or consecutive days if you are moving from one Tampa home to another.
Q5: What if my landlord still deducts for cleaning after I hired a professional?
Take timestamped photos of every room after the professional cleaning and before handing over keys. If the landlord attempts to deduct for cleaning despite the property being professionally cleaned, those photos are your evidence in a dispute. Florida law requires landlords to provide an itemized list of deductions, and if the cleaning claim contradicts your photographic evidence, you have grounds to contest the deduction.
