1.1 Page Purpose
New Tampa is the tenth neighborhood page and targets a 24-square-mile area northeast of Tampa that was annexed by the city in 1988 and has since developed into one of Tampa’s largest and most popular suburban districts. The area is defined by master-planned communities like Tampa Palms, Hunters Green, Pebble Creek, Grand Hampton, Live Oak Preserve, and West Meadows. The population consists primarily of upper-middle-income families and professionals with strong ties to the nearby University of South Florida and the I-75/I-275 corridor employment centers. The median individual income exceeds $99,000, homes are predominantly 2000s-era construction with modern floor plans, and the area features extensive parks and nature preserves including Flatwoods Wilderness Park and New Tampa Nature Park.

3.2 Neighborhood-Specific Content – New Tampa [UNIQUE]
  SECTION ID: #about-new-tampa  
H2 Heading
Experienced Cleaning for New Tampa’s Established Master-Planned Communities
New Tampa’s story is one of deliberate community building. When the city of Tampa annexed this 24-square-mile area in 1988, the land was mostly ranch pastures and undeveloped acreage. Over the following two decades, some of Tampa’s most thoughtfully designed master-planned communities took shape: Tampa Palms arrived with Mediterranean-inspired homes along a signature golf course; Hunters Green added its own championship course surrounded by upscale single-family homes; Pebble Creek, Grand Hampton, and Live Oak Preserve built family-oriented neighborhoods with community pools, playgrounds, and walking trails woven through conservation areas. Today, New Tampa is fully built out and its communities have the mature landscaping, established homeowner associations, and neighborhood identity that newer developments like Wesley Chapel and Riverview are still growing into.
The homes in New Tampa are predominantly 1990s and 2000s construction – newer than Carrollwood or Temple Terrace but more established than Riverview’s 2010s-era communities. This means the housing stock sits at the intersection of modern floor plans and early signs of maintenance maturity: granite countertops that may need resealing, tile grout that has absorbed 15 to 20 years of Florida humidity, screened pool enclosures with aging frames that allow more debris through, and HVAC systems that have been circulating pollen and dust for two decades. Our cleaning team understands this transitional age of home – still modern in layout and materials, but starting to show the accumulation that comes with years of Florida weather and family life.
New Tampa’s geography places many homes adjacent to conservation areas, nature preserves, and the extensive trail systems that residents love. Flatwoods Wilderness Park and New Tampa Nature Park attract hikers, cyclists, and dog walkers who track trail debris, sand, and organic matter back into their homes. The area’s natural setting also means higher pollen counts, more insect activity, and the Florida wildlife (lizards, frogs, spiders) that finds its way into screened enclosures and occasionally into the home itself. Our team addresses these nature-adjacent cleaning challenges as part of every visit – paying extra attention to entry points, lanai transitions, and the surfaces where outdoor debris accumulates.
The demographics of New Tampa create a strong market for recurring cleaning services. The area attracts upper-middle-income professionals and families, many of whom commute via I-75 or I-275 to Tampa’s employment hubs or the USF campus. With children in school, both parents working, and weekends filled with sports and community activities, professional house cleaning is not a luxury in New Tampa – it is a practical necessity for maintaining the standard that these well-kept neighborhoods expect.

3.7 Frequently Asked Questions
  SECTION ID: #faq  
H2 Heading
House Cleaning Questions from New Tampa Residents
Q1: Do you serve all of New Tampa?
Yes. We clean homes in all New Tampa master-planned communities including Tampa Palms, Hunters Green, Pebble Creek, Grand Hampton, Live Oak Preserve, West Meadows, Easton Park, Arbor Greene, Heritage Isles, and surrounding neighborhoods. We cover zip code 33647 and adjacent areas. Call (813) 534-9216 to confirm.
Q2: How much does house cleaning cost in New Tampa?
Regular cleaning typically ranges from $130 to $280 per visit depending on home size and frequency. Most New Tampa homes are 3 to 5 bedrooms with 2 to 3 bathrooms, generally falling in the $160 to $240 range for biweekly service. Golf course homes and larger properties in Tampa Palms or Hunters Green may run higher.
Q3: Our home backs up to a nature preserve – can you address the extra pollen and debris?
Absolutely. This is one of the most common concerns among New Tampa homeowners. We pay special attention to sliding glass doors, lanai entry points, and the floors and surfaces nearest to conservation areas. Our professional vacuuming process targets the fine pollen, insect debris, and outdoor particulates that accumulate in nature-adjacent homes.
Q4: Do you clean homes in gated communities?
Yes. Many New Tampa communities including Tampa Palms and Hunters Green are gated. We coordinate gate access during the initial setup – visitor lists, gate codes, or manned gate procedures – so there is never a delay on cleaning day.
Q5: Our home is 20 years old and the grout is looking tired – can you help?
Yes. Many New Tampa homes built in the late 1990s and 2000s have tile grout that has darkened over two decades of Florida humidity and use. Our grout cleaning process uses targeted solutions and agitation brushes to lift embedded stains and restore the original color. This is one of our most popular add-on services in the New Tampa area.
