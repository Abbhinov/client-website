How to Wash a Large Comforter (King, Queen & Down)

By Spin It Up Laundry • Published May 24, 2026 • 6 min read

A comforter is one of the hardest things to wash at home — and one of the most common reasons people visit a laundromat. The short version: you need a machine big enough for the comforter to move freely, and enough dryer capacity to get it bone-dry so it doesn't grow mildew. Here's how to do it right.

Why your home washer can't handle it

A standard home top-loader holds about 12–16 lb. A king comforter, soaked with water, easily exceeds that — and when it's crammed in, it doesn't agitate or rinse properly. You end up with detergent trapped in the fill, uneven cleaning, and a soggy center that never dries. Front-loaders are gentler but still usually too small for king and many queen comforters.

At Spin It Up we run 60–80 lb extra-large washers and two 130 lb commercial machines — the biggest washers in Chicago — so even a thick king comforter has room to tumble. See the full machine lineup on our self-service page.

Which machine size should you choose?

  • Twin/Full comforter: a large (35–50 lb) washer is plenty.
  • Queen comforter: use an extra-large (60–80 lb) washer.
  • King or thick down comforter: go straight to a 130 lb commercial machine — especially if you want to wash it with sheets or pillows.

Step by step

1

Check the care label

Most cotton and synthetic-fill comforters are machine washable. If it says "dry clean only" — common for some silk or wool-filled comforters — don't wash it. Spot-treat any stains before loading.

2

Load it evenly

Arrange the comforter loosely around the drum, not bunched on one side, so the machine stays balanced. Don't add other heavy items that crowd it out.

3

Use less detergent than you think

A small amount of mild detergent is enough. Too much leaves residue trapped in the fill. Wash on a gentle cycle with warm or cold water.

4

Dry low and slow — with dryer balls

Use a large dryer on low or medium heat. Add wool dryer balls (or clean tennis balls) to break up clumps and re-fluff the fill. Down takes the longest — keep going until there are no damp spots.

5

Make sure it's 100% dry

A comforter that's even slightly damp in the center can develop mildew. Pull it out, feel the middle and the seams, and run more time if needed.
