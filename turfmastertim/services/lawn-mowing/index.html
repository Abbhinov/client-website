Why Consistent Mowing Matters in Florida

I see this all the time — someone skips a week, the grass gets tall, then they mow it short to compensate. That's the worst thing you can do for St. Augustine.

1Scalping

Cutting more than one-third of the blade stresses the plant, weakens roots, and opens the door for disease. When you skip and then cut short, you're actively damaging your lawn.

2Weed Invasion

Thin, stressed turf from inconsistent mowing creates bare spots weeds colonize immediately. Once crabgrass or dollarweed takes hold, it's expensive to fix.

3Pest Vulnerability

Overgrown grass harbors chinch bugs and sod webworms. Proper mowing height and frequency reduces pest habitat.

4HOA Fines

Bloomingdale, FishHawk, and most Brandon subdivisions have strict lawn height requirements. Violations start at $25–$100 per occurrence.

How I Adjust Your Mowing Through the Seasons

Florida lawns don't follow a national playbook. Here's what I actually do month-by-month.

Spring (Mar–May)

Transition to weekly. Height stays at 4 inches for root development. Pre-emergent gets layered in via the health program.

Summer (Jun–Aug)

Weekly essential. 3.5–4 inch height. Morning service to avoid afternoon storms. Watch for chinch bugs.

Fall (Sep–Nov)

Gradual slowdown. May extend to 10-day intervals. Watch for fall armyworms after first cool fronts.

Winter (Dec–Feb)

Bi-weekly. Reduced height incrementally. Light touch during semi-dormancy.
